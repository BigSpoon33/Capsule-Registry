## 1. Differentiating Ma Huang from Guizhi in Exterior Syndromes

**Question:**
How would you differentiate between a patient with wind-cold exterior syndrome treated with Ma Huang versus a patient with wind-heat exterior syndrome treated with Guizhi?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Acridity and warmth of Ma Huang and Guizhi
- Channels entered by Ma Huang and Guizhi
- Specific actions and indications of Ma Huang and Guizhi

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Correctly identifying the acridity and warmth of each herb
- Accurately describing the channels entered by each herb
- Properly distinguishing the specific actions and indications of Ma Huang and Guizhi

**Reference Content:**
Ma Huang and Guizhi are both used in treating exterior syndromes, but they differ in their acridity, warmth, and specific actions. Ma Huang is acrid and warm, primarily entering the Lung and Bladder channels, and is indicated for wind-cold exterior syndromes. Guizhi, on the other hand, is also acrid and warm but is primarily used for wind-cold exterior syndromes with sweating, indicating a more harmonizing action.

## 2. Channel Entry and Functional Actions

**Question:**
In what ways do the channel entries of Ma Huang and Chai Hu differ, and how do these differences affect their functional actions?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Channels entered by Ma Huang and Chai Hu
- Functional actions of Ma Huang and Chai Hu

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Correct identification of the channels entered by Ma Huang and Chai Hu
- Proper explanation of how channel entry affects functional actions

**Reference Content:**
Ma Huang primarily enters the Lung and Bladder channels, making it effective for dispelling wind-cold and relieving nasal congestion. Chai Hu, on the other hand, enters the Lung, Large Intestine, Stomach, and Spleen channels, indicating its versatility in treating lesser yang stage exterior syndromes and other conditions affecting these channels.
