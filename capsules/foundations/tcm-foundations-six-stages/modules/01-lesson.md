## Overview

The Six Stage Theory, formulated by Zhang Zhong Jing in his celebrated book "Discussion of Cold-Induced Diseases" (Shang Han Lun), provides a systematic framework for understanding the progression and treatment of diseases caused by exterior pathogenic factors. This theory divides the body into six stages, each with its own specific clinical manifestations, underlying pathologies, and treatment approaches. The theory is fundamental to Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and continues to be a cornerstone of clinical practice today.

## Key Concepts

### Greater Yang Stage

The Greater Yang stage is characterized by the invasion of Wind-Cold. There are two primary patterns within this stage:

1. **Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Wind (Attack of Wind)**:
   - **Clinical Manifestations**: Aversion to cold, headache, stiff neck, floating pulse.
   - **Pathology**: Excess of Wind-Cold, leading to obstruction of the channels.
   - **Treatment**:
     - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Fengchi (GB20) and Baihui (GV20).
     - **Herbal Formula**: Gui Zhi Tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction) to dispel Wind-Cold.

2. **Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold (Attack of Cold)**:
   - **Clinical Manifestations**: Similar to the previous pattern, but with a stronger emphasis on cold symptoms.
   - **Pathology**: Excess of Cold, leading to constriction and obstruction.
   - **Treatment**:
     - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Fengmen (BL12) and Weishu (BL21).
     - **Herbal Formula**: Ma Huang Tang (Ephedra Decoction) to dispel Wind-Cold.

### Channel Patterns

The channel patterns within the Greater Yang stage focus on the primary channels involved in Wind-Cold invasion:

- **Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Wind (Attack of Wind)**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Aversion to cold, headache, stiff neck, floating pulse.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Wind-Cold, leading to obstruction of the channels.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Fengchi (GB20) and Baihui (GV20).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Gui Zhi Tang (Cinnamon Twig Decoction) to dispel Wind-Cold.

- **Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold (Attack of Cold)**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Similar to the previous pattern, but with a stronger emphasis on cold symptoms.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Cold, leading to constriction and obstruction.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Fengmen (BL12) and Weishu (BL21).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Ma Huang Tang (Ephedra Decoction) to dispel Wind-Cold.

### Organ Patterns

The organ patterns within the Greater Yang stage focus on the organs involved in the accumulation of Water and Blood:

- **Accumulation of Water**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Edema, swelling, and discomfort.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Wind-Cold, leading to the accumulation of fluid.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Yinlingquan (SP9) and Zusanli (ST36).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Wu Ling San (Five-Ling Powder) to resolve dampness and promote diuresis.

- **Accumulation of Blood**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Pain, redness, and swelling.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Wind-Cold, leading to the stagnation of blood.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Xuehai (SP10) and Neiguan (PC6).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Da Huang Mu Dan Tang (Big Yellow and Peony Decoction) to clear heat and resolve stasis.

### Bright Yang Stage

The Bright Yang stage, also known as the Yang Ming channel and organ pattern, is characterized by the accumulation of Heat and Dampness:

- **Bright Yang (Yang Ming) Channel Pattern**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Fever, constipation, thirst, and a red tongue.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Heat and Dampness, leading to the obstruction of the Yang Ming channel.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Quchi (LI11) and Neiting (ST44).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Da Cheng Qi Tang (Great Conquering Qi Decoction) to purge Heat and resolve dampness.

- **Bright Yang (Yang Ming) Organ Pattern**:
  - **Clinical Manifestations**: Fever, constipation, thirst, and a red tongue.
  - **Pathology**: Excess of Heat and Dampness, leading to the obstruction of the Yang Ming organ.
  - **Treatment**:
    - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Quchi (LI11) and Neiting (ST44).
    - **Herbal Formula**: Da Cheng Qi Tang (Great Conquering Qi Decoction) to purge Heat and resolve dampness.

### Lesser Yang (Shao Yang) Stage

The Lesser Yang stage is characterized by the imbalance between the Liver and Gallbladder, often presenting with symptoms such as irritability, alternating chills and fever, and flank pain:

- **Clinical Manifestations**: Alternating chills and fever, irritability, and flank pain.
- **Pathology**: Disharmony between the Liver and Gallbladder.
- **Treatment**:
  - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Yanglingquan (GB34) and Taichong (LR3).
  - **Herbal Formula**: Hao Qin Qing Dan Tang (Artemisiae Scopariae and Pinelliae Rhizoma Decoction) to harmonize the Liver and Gallbladder.

### Greater Yin (Tai Yin) Stage

The Greater Yin stage is characterized by the involvement of the Spleen and Stomach, often presenting with symptoms such as vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain:

- **Clinical Manifestations**: Vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
- **Pathology**: Deficiency of Spleen and Stomach Qi.
- **Treatment**:
  - **Acupuncture**: Points such as Pishu (BL20) and Weishu (BL21).
  - **Herbal Formula**: Li Zhong Wan (Atractylodes and Ginseng Pill) to strengthen the Spleen and Stomach.
