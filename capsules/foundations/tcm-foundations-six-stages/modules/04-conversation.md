## 1. Distinguishing Greater Yang Patterns

**Question:**
How would you differentiate between the "Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Wind" and the "Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold" patterns in a patient presenting with aversion to cold and headache?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of each pattern
- Pathological differences between the two patterns
- Treatment principles for each pattern

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Ability to identify the key clinical signs that distinguish between the two patterns
- Understanding of the underlying pathologies and how they influence treatment approaches
- Correct selection of acupuncture points and herbal formulas based on the identified pattern

**Reference Content:**
The Greater Yang stage is characterized by the invasion of Wind-Cold, with two primary patterns: Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Wind and Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold. These patterns share some similar clinical manifestations, such as aversion to cold and headache, but differ in their emphasis on wind or cold symptoms.

## 2. Channel vs. Organ Patterns

**Question:**
A patient presents with edema and swelling. How would you differentiate between the "Accumulation of Water" and the "Accumulation of Blood" patterns, and what would be the appropriate treatment approach for each?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of Accumulation of Water and Accumulation of Blood
- Pathological differences between the two patterns
- Treatment principles for each pattern

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Ability to identify the specific clinical manifestations that distinguish between the Accumulation of Water and Accumulation of Blood patterns
- Understanding of the underlying pathologies and how they influence treatment approaches
- Correct selection of acupuncture points and herbal formulas based on the identified pattern

**Reference Content:**
The organ patterns within the Greater Yang stage focus on the organs involved in the accumulation of Water and Blood. Accumulation of Water is characterized by edema and swelling, while Accumulation of Blood is marked by pain, redness, and swelling. These patterns reflect the different pathological mechanisms and require distinct treatment strategies.

## 3. Transition to Bright Yang Stage

**Question:**
A patient with a history of Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold has not responded to initial treatments. What would you consider as the next stage of the disease progression, and what treatment principles would you apply?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- The concept of disease progression from Greater Yang to Bright Yang
- Characteristics of the Bright Yang stage
- Treatment principles for the Bright Yang stage

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Ability to recognize the progression of the disease from Greater Yang to Bright Yang
- Understanding of the Bright Yang stage and its clinical manifestations
- Correct selection of treatment strategies based on the Bright Yang stage

**Reference Content:**
The Greater Yang stage, characterized by the Invasion of Wind-Cold with Prevalence of Cold, can progress to the Bright Yang stage if the condition is not adequately treated. The Bright Yang stage involves a deeper involvement of the body, potentially leading to more severe symptoms and complications. Treatment in the Bright Yang stage requires a different approach to address the advanced pathology.
