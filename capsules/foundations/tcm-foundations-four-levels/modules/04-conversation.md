## 1. Differentiating Wind-Heat from Summer-Heat

**Question:**
How would you differentiate a case of Wind-Heat from a case of Summer-Heat based on clinical symptoms and underlying pathology?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Symptoms associated with Wind-Heat
- Symptoms associated with Summer-Heat
- Pathological differences between the two conditions

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify distinct clinical signs and symptoms for each condition
- Understanding the underlying pathological mechanisms that differentiate the two

**Reference Content:**
Wind-Heat is characterized by symptoms such as fever, headache, and throat swelling, while Summer-Heat is associated with high temperatures, thirst, irritability, and loss of appetite. The underlying pathologies also differ, with Wind-Heat being an external pathogenic factor affecting the defensive Qi (Wei) level, whereas Summer-Heat is a more intense heat pathogen that invades the Qi (Nutritive Qi) level.

## 2. Identifying the Four Levels of Warm Diseases

**Question:**
In a patient presenting with fever, irritability, and a maculopapular rash, which level of the Four Levels Theory would you suspect and why?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Nutritive Qi (Ying) level symptoms
- Skin rashes as indicators of warm disease progression

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Ability to correlate clinical symptoms with the appropriate level of the Four Levels Theory
- Understanding the significance of skin rashes in diagnosing warm diseases

**Reference Content:**
Maculopapular rashes are common in the Nutritive Qi (Ying) level of warm diseases, indicating that the pathogenic factors have invaded the body fluids and blood. Fever and irritability are also associated with this level, reflecting the disturbance in the body's fluids and blood.

## 3. Treating Defensive Qi (Wei) Level Warm Diseases

**Question:**
A patient presents with chills, fever, and sweating. How would you approach the treatment of this condition according to the Four Levels Theory?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Pathogens affecting the Defensive Qi (Wei) level
- Herbal formulas and acupuncture points for resolving exterior symptoms

**Assessment Criteria:**
- Knowledge of appropriate herbal formulas and acupuncture points for resolving exterior symptoms
- Understanding the therapeutic approach for clearing Wind-Heat and resolving exterior symptoms

**Reference Content:**
Chills, fever, and sweating are indicative of Wind-Heat affecting the Defensive Qi (Wei) level. Treatment should focus on clearing heat and resolving exterior symptoms. Acupuncture points such as Hegu (LI 4) and Fengchi (GB 20) can be used, along with herbal formulas like Chai Ge Jie Ji Tang (Decoction of Chai Hu and Ge Gen) to clear heat and resolve exterior symptoms.
