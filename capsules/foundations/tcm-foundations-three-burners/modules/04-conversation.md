## 1. Differentiating Upper Burner Syndromes

**Question:**
A patient presents with fever, headache, and a runny nose with yellow discharge. They also have a slight cough and body aches. How would you differentiate between Wind-Heat in the Lungs’ Defensive Qi portion and Heat in the Qi level of the Lungs?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi portion
- Clinical manifestations of Heat in the Qi level of the Lungs
- Differences in tongue coating and pulse characteristics

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify key clinical manifestations that distinguish Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi from Heat in the Qi level.
- Correct identification of the appropriate acupuncture and herbal formulas based on the differentiation.

**Reference Content:**
The clinical manifestations of Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi portion include fever, aversion to cold, headache, sore throat, slight sweating, and a runny nose with yellow discharge. The tongue is red in the front or sides with a thin white coating, and the pulse is floating and rapid. In contrast, Heat in the Qi level of the Lungs manifests with persistent fever, severe headache, dry cough, yellow and sticky phlegm, thirst, a red tongue with a yellow coating, and a slippery and rapid pulse.

## 2. Differentiating Middle Burner Syndromes

**Question:**
A patient complains of fever, thirst, and restlessness. They also have a red and dry tongue with a thin and rapid pulse. How would you differentiate between Heat in Bright Yang and Damp-Heat in the Spleen?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of Heat in Bright Yang
- Clinical manifestations of Damp-Heat in the Spleen
- Differences in tongue and pulse characteristics

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify key clinical manifestations that distinguish Heat in Bright Yang from Damp-Heat in the Spleen.
- Correct identification of the appropriate acupuncture and herbal formulas based on the differentiation.

**Reference Content:**
Heat in Bright Yang presents with fever, thirst, restlessness, a red and dry tongue, and a thin and rapid pulse. Damp-Heat in the Spleen, on the other hand, causes fatigue, poor appetite, abdominal distension, yellowish complexion, yellow and sticky stools, puffy eyes, a yellow and greasy tongue coating, and a slippery and rapid pulse.

## 3. Treatment Strategies for Upper Burner Syndromes

**Question:**
A patient is diagnosed with Wind-Heat in the Lungs’ Defensive Qi portion. How would you formulate an acupuncture and herbal treatment plan for this condition?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Acupuncture points for clearing Wind-Heat and invigorating the Defensive Qi
- Herbal formula for the beginning stage of invasions of Wind-Heat

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to correctly identify the appropriate acupuncture points and herbal formula for the treatment of Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi.
- Understanding of the rationale behind the selection of the acupuncture points and herbal formula.

**Reference Content:**
For Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi portion, the acupuncture points to focus on include Hegu (LI4), Fengchi (GB20), and Yujit (LU5). The herbal formula of choice is Lonicera–Forsythia Powder (Yin Qiao San), which is effective for the initial stage of Wind-Heat. This formula helps to clear the Wind-Heat and invigorate the Defensive Qi.
