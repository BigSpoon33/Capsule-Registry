## Overview

The Three Burners theory is a diagnostic framework in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) that categorizes the clinical manifestations of febrile diseases based on their location within the body rather than their energetic depth. This theory was formulated by Wu Ju Tong (1758–1836), author of 'Systematic Differentiation of Warm Diseases' (Wen Bing Tiao Bian). The Three Burners correspond to the Upper, Middle, and Lower Burners, and they are used to identify the location of disease within the body. This lesson will cover the patterns of Wind-Heat in the Upper Burner, Heat in the Middle Burner, and Heat in the Lower Burner.

## Key Concepts

### Upper Burner

#### Wind-Heat in the Lungs’ Defensive Qi portion

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Fever
  - Aversion to cold
  - Headache
  - Sore throat
  - Slight sweating
  - Runny nose with yellow discharge
  - Swollen tonsils
  - Body aches
  - Slight thirst
  - Tongue: Red in the front or sides with a thin white coating
  - Pulse: Floating and rapid

- **Pathology**: 
  - The pathology of Wind-Heat invading the Defensive Qi portion of the Lungs is the same as that for the Defensive Qi level of the Four Levels. The tongue coating is white because the pathogenic factor is still on the exterior.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Wind-Heat and invigorate the Defensive Qi.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Lonicera–Forsythia Powder (Yin Qiao San) is the formula of choice for the beginning stage of invasions of Wind-Heat.

#### Heat in the Lungs (Qi level)

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Similar to Wind-Heat in the Defensive Qi, but more severe.
  - Persistent fever
  - Severe headache
  - Dry cough
  - Yellow and sticky phlegm
  - Thirst
  - Tongue: Red with a yellow coating
  - Pulse: Slippery and rapid

- **Pathology**: 
  - The Qi level of the Lungs is invaded by Heat, leading to symptoms of coughing and phlegm production.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Heat from the Qi level and regulate the Lungs.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Mahuang Xingren Ganshi Decoction (Ma Xing Shi Gan Tang) is commonly used.

#### Heat in the Pericardium (Nutritive Qi level)

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - High fever
  - Severe headache
  - Delirium
  - Confusion
  - Red and dry tongue
  - Thin and rapid pulse

- **Pathology**: 
  - Heat invades the Pericardium, disturbing the Heart and causing confusion and delirium.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Heat from the Heart and Pericardium.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Qingying Decoction (Qing Ying Tang) is the formula of choice.

### Middle Burner

#### Heat in Bright Yang

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Fever
  - Thirst
  - Restlessness
  - Red and dry tongue
  - Thin and rapid pulse

- **Pathology**: 
  - Heat invades the Bright Yang, leading to symptoms of restlessness and confusion.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Heat and regulate the Middle Burner.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Qingwen Baidu Decoction (Qingwen Baidu Tang) is commonly used.

#### Damp-Heat in the Spleen

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Fatigue
  - Poor appetite
  - Abdominal distension
  - Yellowish complexion
  - Yellow and sticky stools
  - Puffy eyes
  - Tongue: Yellow and greasy coating
  - Pulse: Slippery and rapid

- **Pathology**: 
  - Damp-Heat in the Spleen leads to digestive issues and yellowish discoloration.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Damp-Heat and regulate the Spleen.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Huoxiang Zhengqi San is commonly used.

### Lower Burner

#### Heat in the Kidneys

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Fever
  - Night sweats
  - Red and dry tongue
  - Thin and rapid pulse

- **Pathology**: 
  - Heat invades the Kidneys, leading to symptoms of night sweats and dryness.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Heat from the Kidneys.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Liuwei Dihuang Wan is commonly used.

#### Liver-Heat stirs Wind

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Severe headache
  - Dizziness
  - Convulsions
  - Red and dry tongue
  - Thin and rapid pulse

- **Pathology**: 
  - Liver-Heat stirs Wind, leading to symptoms of convulsions and dizziness.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that clear Liver-Heat and calm the Wind.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Chuanxiong Cha Tiao San is commonly used.

#### Liver Empty-Wind

- **Clinical Manifestations**: 
  - Dizziness
  - Blurred vision
  - Dryness and itching in the eyes
  - Red and dry tongue
  - Thin and rapid pulse

- **Pathology**: 
  - Liver Empty-Wind, leading to symptoms of dizziness and blurred vision.

- **Treatment**: 
  - **Acupuncture**: Focus on points that nourish the Liver and calm the Wind.
  - **Herbal Formula**: Qiju Dihuang Wan is commonly used.

## Clinical Relevance

Understanding the Three Burners theory is crucial for TCM practitioners as it provides a framework for diagnosing and treating febrile diseases. By identifying the location of the disease within the body, practitioners can tailor their treatment strategies more effectively. For instance, treating Wind-Heat in the Upper Burner involves clearing Wind-Heat, whereas addressing Damp-Heat in the Middle Burner involves regulating the Spleen and clearing Dampness. This knowledge is essential for formulating appropriate acupuncture points and herbal formulas, which can significantly improve patient outcomes.
