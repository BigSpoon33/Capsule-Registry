## 1. Governing Vessel (Du Mai) vs. Directing Vessel (Ren Mai)

**Question:**
A patient presents with a headache, fever, and dizziness. How would you differentiate between a pattern involving the Governing Vessel (Du Mai) and a pattern involving the Directing Vessel (Ren Mai)?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of the Governing Vessel (Du Mai)
- Clinical manifestations of the Directing Vessel (Ren Mai)
- Connecting channels of the Governing Vessel (Du Mai)
- Connecting channels of the Directing Vessel (Ren Mai)

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify key clinical manifestations for both vessels
- The understanding of the connecting channels and their implications for treatment

**Reference Content:**
The Governing Vessel (Du Mai) is associated with symptoms such as fever, headache, dizziness, and mental agitation. It connects with the spine, marrow, and brain. In contrast, the Directing Vessel (Ren Mai) is linked to gynecological disorders, lower back pain, and infertility.

## 2. Governing Vessel (Du Mai) vs. Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai)

**Question:**
A patient with menstrual disorders, uterine bleeding, and lower abdominal pain comes to you. How would you differentiate between a pattern involving the Governing Vessel (Du Mai) and the Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai)?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Clinical manifestations of the Governing Vessel (Du Mai)
- Clinical manifestations of the Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai)
- Treatment approaches for both vessels

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to distinguish between the clinical manifestations of the Governing Vessel and the Penetrating Vessel
- The understanding of appropriate treatment strategies for each vessel

**Reference Content:**
The Governing Vessel (Du Mai) is associated with symptoms such as headache, dizziness, and mental agitation. The Penetrating Vessel (Chong Mai) is linked to menstrual disorders, uterine bleeding, and lower abdominal pain. Both vessels are treated with acupuncture points and herbs that tonify the uterus and regulate qi.

## 3. Combined Directing and Penetrating Vessel Patterns

**Question:**
A patient presents with unstable menstrual cycles, lower abdominal pain, and gynecological disorders. How would you differentiate and treat the combined pattern of the Directing and Penetrating Vessels?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Patterns of combined Directing and Penetrating Vessels
- Clinical manifestations of the combined pattern
- Treatment strategies for the combined pattern

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify the specific combined patterns of the Directing and Penetrating Vessels
- The understanding of appropriate treatment strategies, including acupuncture points and herbs

**Reference Content:**
The combined pattern of the Directing and Penetrating Vessels can manifest as unstable menstrual cycles, lower abdominal pain, and gynecological disorders. Treatment strategies include using acupuncture points like Ren4 (Guanyuan), Ren6 (Qihai), Chong1 (Chong Mai), and Sanyinjiao (SP6), as well as herbs such as Dang Gui (Angelica sinensis), Wu Wei Zi (Schisandra chinensis), and Chuan Xiong (Ligusticum chuanxiong).
