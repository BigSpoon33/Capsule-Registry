## 1. Differentiating Channels from Organs

**Question:**
How would you differentiate a pathology of the Lung channel from a pathology of the Lung organ in a patient presenting with cough and wheezing?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- The organ versus channel distinction
- Symptoms and clinical manifestations of channel pathology

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify symptoms that specifically indicate channel pathology
- The understanding that channel pathology often manifests externally, whereas organ pathology is more internal

**Reference Content:**
Problems within the organs can affect the channels, and vice versa. However, it is crucial to distinguish between issues affecting only the channels and those that penetrate into the organs. The main channel of the Lung is associated with respiratory function and the regulation of body fluids. The connecting channel of the Lung can be either empty or full, indicating different pathological states.

## 2. Identifying Main Channel Pathology

**Question:**
A patient presents with diarrhea and abdominal pain. How would you differentiate between pathology of the Large Intestine's main channel and pathology of the Stomach's main channel?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Main channel functions for the Large Intestine and Stomach
- Clinical manifestations associated with the main channels

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify the specific functions and clinical manifestations of the Large Intestine's main channel (elimination of waste) and the Stomach's main channel (digestion and transformation of food)
- The understanding that diarrhea and abdominal pain can be related to the main channel of the Large Intestine, while other symptoms might suggest the main channel of the Stomach

**Reference Content:**
- **Main Channel**: The Large Intestine is responsible for the elimination of waste, while the Stomach is involved in the digestion and transformation of food.
- **Clinical Manifestations**: Diarrhea and abdominal pain are more commonly associated with the Large Intestine's main channel.

## 3. Understanding Connecting Channel States

**Question:**
A patient is experiencing chest pain and palpitations. How would you differentiate between a pathology of the Heart's connecting channel and a pathology of the Pericardium's connecting channel?

**Key Concepts to Cover:**
- Connecting channel functions for the Heart and Pericardium
- Clinical manifestations associated with the connecting channels

**Assessment Criteria:**
- The ability to identify the specific functions and clinical manifestations of the Heart's connecting channel (blood circulation and mental functions) and the Pericardium's connecting channel (protection of the heart)
- The understanding that chest pain and palpitations can be related to the connecting channel of the Heart, while other symptoms might suggest the connecting channel of the Pericardium

**Reference Content:**
The Heart's connecting channel can be either empty or full, indicating different pathological states, and is associated with blood circulation and mental functions. The Pericardium's connecting channel can also be either empty or full, indicating different pathological states, and is associated with the protection of the heart.
